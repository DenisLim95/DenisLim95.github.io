AI Safety Research

Broadly, I'm interested in misalignment, and in particular in failures that current evaluations miss because they only look at model outputs. My work uses mechanistic interpretability to study what models represent internally. It runs along three lines. The first is representation geometry, meaning how features are organized in a model's activations and weights and what that organization tells us about which features are stable and which are not (Lim et al., 2026). The second is model self-knowledge, meaning whether a transformer can detect its own internal errors, how reinforcement learning changes this, and how well a model's stated uncertainty matches its internal uncertainty (with Laura Ying Schulz at SPAR, and at the KAIST STAI Lab). The third is interpretability for multi-agent systems, including covert communication between agents through activations (Rangaraju et al., 2026) and the mechanisms behind overconfidence in group deliberation (Batra et al., in submission).

I'm a research intern at the KAIST STAI Lab with Seong Joon Oh and a research fellow at SPAR. Before that I was a research engineer at Arcadia Impact, where I worked with UK AISI on automating alignment research, and a fellow in the MARS programme at the Cambridge AI Safety Hub. I worked as an engineer for several years before moving into research, most recently at Scale AI and Two Sigma. I studied mechanical engineering and computer science at USC.
